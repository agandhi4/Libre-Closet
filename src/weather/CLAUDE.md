# Weather

`src/weather/` (pure) and `src/web/weather/` (#14, plan section 7). **`WEATHER_ENABLED=false`** (default true): `createApp()` builds no service, so there is no `/weather` route (404), no weather on any page or in the profile, no MCP weather tool, and the seed stores no location: nothing is fetched and no location is stored (`weather.spec.ts`, "weather off").

- **Location** (`user_weather`, `drizzle/0015_weather.sql`; a row once the user sets something, cascading with the user): a **home** city picked from the geocoding search (`home_name`, `home_latitude`, `home_longitude`) and the installed app's **"Use my location"** (`here_*`, `here_located_at`), which stands in for home for `HERE_FRESH_HOURS` (12), then home again. Every coordinate is rounded to 2 decimals (about 1 km) by `roundedLocation` (`src/weather/location.ts`) before it is stored, used as the cache key or sent; the phone rounds too (`public/js/locate.js`), so its precise position never leaves it. Check constraints keep each group whole and on the globe. One writer per group (`queries.ts`).
- **Provider: Open-Meteo, from the server only** (`open-meteo.ts`): the forecast (16 days: hourly apparent temperature, chance of precipitation and weather code; daily high, low, code) requested with `timezone=APP_TIMEZONE`, so its days are calendar days and its hours the occasions' hours (`OCCASION_HINTS`, which are APP_TIMEZONE hours; the phone's location in another zone reads in the household's); the geocoding search for the city. Through the outbound fetcher with `hosts` (Request security, Outbound fetches); the request carries the rounded coordinates (or the typed city) and nothing about the user. Answers are checked against TypeBox schemas before use. The calendar shows nothing past the forecast.
- **Cache** (`weather_forecast`, `service.ts`): one row per rounded location, no user id: the last good forecast (normalized, `jsonb`), `fetched_at`, `attempted_at`. Fresh for an hour; **past that the cached answer is served at once and refreshed in the background** (#114: the first Today, Ideas or Shuffle after the hour waited 0.4-10 s for Open-Meteo inside the request); **only a cold miss** (no answer kept) waits, for at most the fetcher's 10 s. A failed refresh keeps the last good answer and waits 10 minutes before asking again. One refresh per location at a time per process, and the row is not read while one runs: an ask meanwhile is served what the refresh started from (a cold one joins it), so an ask that read the stale row just before the refresh saved can never fetch again. A page is the same whether the refresh succeeds, fails or is still running; the next ask shows the new answer. That discipline is `createLocationCache` (`location-cache.ts`, `location-cache.spec.ts`), which both caches use (the normals too): a new Open-Meteo answer kept per location goes through it rather than a copy. A background refresh never rejects (a failure, even recording it, is logged), and **the app's close awaits `WeatherService.settled()`** before ending the pool, so a refresh in flight still saves its row. No timer: a refresh starts only when something asks. Integration specs that count fetches after a stale ask wait for the row's `attempted_at` first (`refreshed` in `weather.spec.ts`, `climate-normals.spec.ts`). **A table, not memory** (the plan said memory): the hourly autoupdate and restarts start warm, two servers in an overlapping deploy share it, and the last good answer outlives a restart while Open-Meteo is down.
- **Where it shows** (`views.tsx`, the redesign's one `WeatherLine` and one `WeatherDay`): `WeatherSlot` in the page header (the wardrobe, above and outside `#wardrobe-main` so filtering never reloads it, and not in select mode or the picker; the calendar, above the week; Today, under the date) loads `GET /weather/summary` once in place (`hx-trigger="load"`, its own `hx-indicator`): today's line (icon, low to high in the user's unit, "rain from 3 pm", "take a layer", the place and "as of 8:31 PM") or, without a location, the link to set one; with `?from=&to=` the calendar's day chips arrive out of band into each day's `WeatherDaySlot`. **Pages never render the forecast themselves**: a tab root must render byte for byte the same while nothing changes (PWA, stale-while-revalidate), and an hourly forecast would mark it changed. Offline the worker answers the fragment from its cache (`url|hx`) and "as of" says how old it is (`test/weather.spec.ts`). The settings are `WeatherSettings`, a section of the profile (the redesign's Profile slots it in unchanged).
- **Matching** (`src/weather/match.ts`, pure, `match.spec.ts` across seasons and occasions; for the generator #9, Today #15 and the MCP tools): `weatherNeeds(day, occasion, offset)` reads the occasion's window, its feels-like range plus the personal offset, and sets targets: the **torso** (top or one-piece plus layer, combined 1 to 9: one step per 4 °C down from 26 °C; a tee is 2, a tee under a jacket 5, a sweater under a parka 9) and **legs and feet** (one garment, 1 to 5); **a layer** when the window's coolest and warmest hours are two torso steps apart (then the outfit is judged with the layer at the coolest hours and without it at the warmest; a swing inside the heat asks for nothing); **rain** when an hour of the window reaches 50 %, wanting a water-resistant layer, footwear or accessory (a bag's does not count). `assessOutfit(needs, garments)` lists the problems and a score (0 fits) and judges only the parts an outfit has; `matchGarment` counts an unknown warmth as the type's preset (its weight step included), else the role's usual. Availability (`isAvailable`) is a separate question: filter, then match.
- **Personal offset and units** (`temperature.ts`): °C added to every feels-like, within ±5, moved half a degree by "I was too warm" / "I was too cold" on the profile (`nudgeTemperatureOffset`, one SQL statement so quick taps all count; the writer #9's "say why not" calls). Everything is computed and stored in °C; `temperature_unit` (°F by default, for the NYC household; the constant and the migration default must agree) is only how the user reads it.
- **Routes** (session; `weather/routes.tsx`): `GET /weather/summary[?from=&to=]` (always a fragment; both or neither, at most 42 days, else 400), `GET /weather/places?q=` (2 to 100 characters; `WEATHER_SEARCH_LIMIT`, 20 a minute per user; `secretPath`, so the request log never shows the typed city; a failed search is a 200 message so htmx shows it), `POST /weather/home`, `/home/clear`, `/here`, `/here/clear`, `/unit`, `/feedback`, `/offset/reset` (htmx: the part of the section each changed, never the whole, which holds the unit's `AutosaveForm`: the location forms `#weather-location`, the feedback `#weather-offset`, the unit its status line and the offset out of band; a plain post: 303 to `/auth/profile#weather`; `data-needs-network`).
- **Logs** (context `Weather`): a refresh's start (`Forecast for 40.69,-73.98: refreshing; serving the one from <instant> meanwhile`, or `none kept, the ask waits`), its outcome `Forecast for 40.69,-73.98: 16 days in 120 ms` or a failure with its reason and what is served instead, `Place search: 1 result(s) in 80 ms`: the rounded location, never the user or the query. The settings routes log the user (context `Web`), never a location; the fetcher's line names the host only.
- **Climate normals** (`src/weather/normals.ts`, pure, `normals.spec.ts`; `drizzle/0025_weather_normals.sql`): what a calendar day is usually like at a place, for **a trip's days past the forecast only**. From Open-Meteo's **historical archive** (`archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive`, ERA5 reanalysis: what the weather was), not its climate API (CMIP6 model runs: a model's simulated days, biased and model-specific): one request for `NORMAL_YEARS` (10) whole years before the household's current one (`normalYears`: 2016-2025 all through 2026, New Year's Eve in New York included) of daily air high and low, feels-like high and low and precipitation, at the rounded location, in `APP_TIMEZONE` like the forecast (about 140 KB under the 512 KB cap; Open-Meteo weighs it as ~260 of its free calls). `climateNormals` averages each calendar day over the days `NORMAL_WINDOW_DAYS` (7) either side in every year (about 150 observations, wrapping New Year; 29 February kept); the rain chance is the share of wet days (`WET_DAY_MM`, 1 mm, the WMO's rain day). Cached in **`weather_normals`**, a table beside `weather_forecast` rather than a kind in it (another endpoint, answer and lifetime; a shared row would need a kind in its key and a payload typed by it), **one row per location holding all 366 days**, not one per month: the archive answers a date range, so any month's normals cost the ten years' fetch anyway. Fresh for 30 days (`NORMALS_FRESH_FOR_MS`: the years change on New Year, the archive's newest days settle over weeks), the same last-good, 10-minute retry and single-flight (`normalsFor`). **The typical day** (`typicalDay`) turns a day's normals into a `DayForecast` so `weatherNeeds`/`assessOutfit` judge it with the same inputs: 24 hours whose feels-like follow a clear day's curve (`diurnalPhase`, shared with the seed's simulated forecasts) from the normal low at 6:00 to the high at 15:00, every hour's precipitation chance the day's rain chance (so water resistance is asked only where most days are wet), read as household hours like the forecast's, a condition of only dry, rain or snow. The approximation is the normal's middle, not a day's spread: a real day may be warmer, colder or wetter. **Never for Today, the calendar, the gallery's own days, the weekly plan or its re-plan**: those stay forecast-only (a typical day must never swap a planned outfit); only `tripForecast` and `ideasFor` with a trip's `place` ask for normals (`climate-normals.spec.ts` checks Today, a far gallery day and "Plan my week" fetch no archive; `week-plan.spec.ts`'s fake service rejects `normalsFor`). Logs: `Climate normals for 30.27,-97.74: 366 days from 2016-2025 in 140 ms`, a failure like the forecast's.
- **Trips (#10)**: a trip's forecast is its destination through the same geocoding and `forecastFor`, for its days within the 16 days, and its climate normals past them (`tripForecast`; see Trips); `ideasFor` takes the destination as `place`. `get_weather` stays the user's own location; `get_trip` carries the trip's.
- **Tests never call Open-Meteo**: `test/support/weather-stub.ts` (an HTTP server on 127.0.0.2, admitted by the weather fetcher's own policy) serves the seed's simulated New York weather from today in the requested zone, and the same simulated days for the archive's years (`archiveAnswer`), so the normals are New York's as the seed models them. The harness runs with `WEATHER_ENABLED=false` and refuses to boot a spec that turns it on without the stub; the e2e test server always starts it.

## Layout

```
  weather/             The weather, pure (#14; see Weather): location.ts (rounding to ~1 km, the only
                       form a coordinate is kept or sent in), temperature.ts (units, the personal
                       offset), forecast.ts (the forecast shape, WMO conditions, a clear day's
                       curve), match.ts (what a day asks of an outfit for an occasion, and how an
                       outfit answers it), normals.ts (climate normals and the typical day, for a
                       trip's days past the forecast)
```

## Gotchas

- **Weather is off in the integration harness.** `BASE_ENV` sets `WEATHER_ENABLED=false`; a spec that turns it on must pass `weather: (await startWeatherStub()).options`, or `createTestApp` refuses to boot (a spec must never reach Open-Meteo). The e2e test server always serves the stand-in.
- **Climate normals are a trip's far days' alone.** `WeatherService.normalsFor` is asked only by `tripForecast` and by `ideasFor` for a trip's `place` past the forecast. Today, the calendar, the gallery's own days and the weekly plan and its re-plan must stay forecast-only: a typical day is not a forecast, and the re-plan swaps outfits when the weather changes. A new caller of `normalsFor` labels what it shows as typical.
- **The weather's days and hours are the household's, not the location's.** The forecast is requested in `APP_TIMEZONE`, so "today", the calendar's chips and the occasion windows line up; derive them with `todayIn`/`hourIn`, never `toISOString()` or UTC getters. The integration spec pins the clock (`vi.useFakeTimers({ toFake: ['Date'] })`) at 00:30 UTC on a Sunday, still Saturday evening in New York.
