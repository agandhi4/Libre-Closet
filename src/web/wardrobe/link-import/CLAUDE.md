# Link import

Part of the Wardrobe (`src/web/wardrobe/CLAUDE.md`).

- **Link import** (#6, `link-import/`: `routes.tsx`, `import.ts` the fetch and the pending photo, `prefill.ts` the form values, `link-page.tsx`, `photo-choice.tsx`, and the extractor): `extractProduct(html, pageUrl)` (`extract.ts`, pure) turns a product page into form prefills: schema.org Product JSON-LD (also inside `@graph`, arrays, a WebPage's `mainEntity`, a ProductGroup's variants), then Open Graph and `product:` tags, then `<title>`, each field from the first source that has it; price as a decimal string with its ISO currency; up to 8 image candidates, absolute (against `<base href>`), http(s) only, each once. `guess.ts` maps words to the form's values: colours onto `GARMENT_COLORS` ("navy" is blue), category and type from the name (the phrase that ends last wins: "shirt dress" is a dress), materials, a stated weight ("240 gsm", "14 oz") to `fabricWeight`, and "heavyweight"/"lightweight" without a number to the type's weight-step warmth. `html.ts` is a linear tag scanner, not a parser: the page is a stranger's, so nothing may throw, backtrack or recurse on it (`extract.spec.ts` feeds it broken and hostile pages).
  - **The flow**: "Add from a link" on the new garment form (or Android's share sheet) opens the link page; it fetches nothing until Fetch (a GET never writes). `importLink` (`import.ts`) fetches with `accept: ['html', 'image']`: an image is stored as the **pending photo**; a page goes through `extractProduct`, then up to `MAX_PHOTO_CHOICES` (6) of its image candidates are fetched together, each turned into a small WebP preview in memory (`Photos.preview`), and the first readable one stored as the pending photo. `importedForm` (`prefill.ts`, pure) makes the form values: the category's presets fill what the page did not say (as `withPresets`), a stated weight shows in gsm, the price only when in dollars or without a currency (else a notice names it), the product link is the link as given. The answer is the ordinary new-garment form (posting to `POST /wardrobe`) with notices, the pending photo (hidden `linkPhoto` plus its thumb) and, for two or more readable candidates, the choices as buttons with `data:` previews. A tap posts `url` and the current `linkPhoto` (`hx-params`) to the photo route, which stores the pick and discards the photo it replaces. A page with nothing extractable opens the form with the link kept and a message. `link-import.spec.ts` serves fixture pages from 127.0.0.2 (`test/integration/link-sites.ts`).
  - **A pending photo** is bytes without a `file` row plus a `pending_photo` row naming the user who fetched it (a MANAGE grantee's import is theirs until saved), recorded through `keepPendingPhoto` with the per-user cap (see Images), as the add sheet's upload is. The save claims it: `createGarmentWithPendingPhoto` (`writes.ts`) takes the pending row (the requester's only), inserts the `file` row (queued for its cutout, owned by the wardrobe's owner) and the garment in one transaction, and refuses (the form again, "no longer available") a name with no pending row of the requester's, no bytes, or a `file` row already. `discardPendingPhoto` deletes only the requester's own pending photo. Nothing reaches `garment` or `file` before the save; reconciliation removes a pending photo a day old with its row, outside the deletion guard.
  - **Memory**: one import holds up to 6 fetched images of up to 15 MB at once, about 90 MB transient, plus their preview decodes. `LINK_IMPORT_LIMIT` bounds one user's rate; concurrent users multiply it.

## Gotchas

- **The form's `linkPhoto` is a name the client sent: the `pending_photo` row is the capability, not the name.** Claiming (`createGarmentWithPendingPhoto`) and discarding (`discardPendingPhoto`) both require a pending row for that name **and the requesting user**, so knowing another user's pending name (or a saved photo's) gets nothing. `commitWithPhoto` deletes the new bytes when its transaction fails, which is right only for bytes the same request wrote: the claim never deletes bytes, and the discard deletes them only after taking its own pending row. Both also take `lockPhotoName` (`src/web/files/queries.ts`, a transaction advisory lock on the name) and check that no `file` row exists; the pending row's delete orders a save, a discard, an eviction and reconciliation, so exactly one of them wins.
- **Link import's photo choices are never stored.** Only the pick is: the candidates are previews inline as `data:` URLs (the CSP's `img-src` allows `data:` and no other origin, which is also why remote images cannot simply be shown), and the photo a pick replaces is discarded at once. With the per-user cap of 10 pending photos, one user can never hold more than 10 stored imports.
- **The share target is a GET that fetches nothing.** Android opens `/wardrobe/new/from-link?title=&text=&url=` (the link arrives in either `url` or `text`; `linkIn` takes the first http(s) link). Fetching stores a pending photo, and a GET never writes; a POST share target would also need its Origin to pass the same-origin check. The person taps Fetch.
