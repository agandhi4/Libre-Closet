# Styling

`src/web/styling/` (#42; redesign section 3 "Styling"): the outfit composer that replaced the builder. **One row per role, top to toe** (`OUTFIT_ORDER`: layer, one-piece, top, bottom, footwear, accessory, bag, and `none`: `other` and custom categories), each a scroll-snap strip with its neighbours peeking, "No garment" first, then the addressed wardrobe's closet garments of that role (`inCloset`, the capsule's with `?capsule=`), newest first: the builder's cycle, by role. The centred item is the row's choice.

- **The engine is pure** (`rows.ts`, `rows.spec.ts`): rows are states (`role`, `garmentId`, `locked`). `freshStates` opens the dock's stack: the newest of each worn role, accessories, bags, `none` and a dress beside separates empty; **never the day's seed or anything that changes by itself**, so bare `/styling` is byte-stable and a stale-while-revalidate tab root (`OPENS_STALE`; `delivery.spec.ts`). `savedStates` (`?outfit=`: a row per garment, two accessories are two rows, an empty row per other role), `lockedOn` (`?with=`), `shuffledStates` (the generator's idea in the unlocked drawn rows, `DRAWN_ROLES`: a role missing from the idea goes to "No garment", a one-piece empties top and bottom; locked rows and accessory, bag and `none` rows are left), `stylingRows` (each state with its window; an archived garment an edited outfit holds is shown first, marked, and kept on save; a deleted one falls back to "No garment").
- **Reads** (`queries.ts`, scalar subqueries read together by `reads.ts`; #163): `roleWindowsSql` is the windows whatever the wardrobe holds: window functions rank and count each role (`categoryRole` as a SQL `case`, from `builtInCategoriesOf`) and return `STRIP_PAGE` (10) garments past the deepest chosen one per role; a strip's sentinel (`intersect`, a horizontal scroller) fetches `GET /styling/garments?role=&before=<id>` (keyset). **Chosen is judged in SQL as `(garment, row's role)` pairs** (`Chosen`), so a posted garment reaches its strip exactly when `checkedStates` keeps it (the wardrobe's, of that role; a recategorised one stretches nothing), and `?outfit=` reaches its garments through the outfit's id. `savedOutfitSql` (an outfit, its owner's), `rowGarmentsSql` (what posted rows may carry: see Include picks), capsules' `capsuleRefSql` (the `?capsule=` check). **Statements** (production pays ~114 ms each, #156; `styling-statements.spec.ts` pins them): after the session (which reads a grantee's share with it, #170), `stripsReads` is one `selectScalars` statement with the checks, the windows, the capsule menu and a grantee's switcher name: the page without `?with=`, "Add row" and a refused Save's page are that one statement (a trip's `aimIdeas` beside it). "Style this" and Shuffle need two, as the windows must reach the idea: `ideaReads` first (the checks, the posted rows' garments and the lockable ones, `styledGarmentsSql`, with ideasFor's own statement through `ideasColumns`/`ideasFrom`, or over a share `closetGarmentsSql` for the pure `browseIdea`), then `stripsReads`. The checks ride in the statement and are judged after it (`checkedCapsule`, `checkedOutfit`, `checkedStates`): reading before a capsule check is safe, since `inCapsule` matches nothing of another wardrobe. A grantee's statements never name `garment_wear`, `outfit_slot` or `generator_avoid` (the locked garments are read without their last-worn day). The price: with more than one bad parameter the refusal named may differ from before (a rows 400 now comes before a capsule 404; a trip's 404 before the capsule's on Shuffle and "Style this"); each alone refuses as it did.
- **Include picks** (`?picks=1`, #335 part B, the Muse epic; `docs/plans/2026-10-05-muse-suggestions.md` §4 E; it replaced the plan's `?plan=`, #273): one toggle, `PicksToggle` in the header, a boosted link (`role="switch"`) flipping `picks` in the URL, off by default, so the bare page stays byte-stable. **Never over a shared wardrobe nor with a destination** (`scopeOf`: `?for=` plans or packs the outfit, which a piece to buy would refuse), where it is neither shown nor read. On, the garments offered to style with (`picksSql`: `offeredToStyle`, still wanted and not under a need set aside, the owner's own wishlist items too) join their role's strip **first**, badged "To buy" (`data-to-buy`), **a need's options side by side** (by need, newest first, then Muse's rank; then lone picks and own items, newest first). They are a window's `picks` (`withPicks`, `rows.ts`), not the cycle: `count` and `moreBefore` ignore them, `freshStates` never opens on one, a role the closet lacks still gets a row. They ride in `stripsReads`' one statement (`styling-statements.spec.ts`). **What posted rows may carry is one rule, `rowGarmentsSql`** (`RowCarry`, from the scope by `carryOf`): owned garments; with picks on, also what `offeredToStyle` offers; while editing a saved outfit, any of the owner's. Every read of posted rows (the page, Shuffle, "Add row", a refused Save's page) and the Save's own read (`carriedGarments`) use it, so a chosen pick survives every round trip (Shuffle never draws one; a locked one stays). **Save with picks on** (`picks` posted) is a slot write, `saveWithPicks` through `createOutfit` (not `pickTo`): the outfit is incomplete until its pieces are bought (Outfits, Incomplete outfits), once however often posted; with the sheet's day an incomplete one is refused (`OutfitIncomplete`, the page again with its pieces and "Bought it" links, rows kept); with a destination posted, a 400. An edit's Save carries any of the owner's garments, picks on or not. The outfit page's Edit in Styling opens an incomplete outfit with `picks=1`. Behaviour: `styling-picks.spec.ts` (integration and Playwright).
- **The browser** (the shared snap strip, `src/web/strip/CLAUDE.md`: `SnapStrip`/`snapItem` in `styling-row.tsx`, the observer in `public/js/snap-strip.js`; `public/js/styling.js` adds the locks through the observer's `snap-strip:choose` and `snap-strip:watch` events; an inline module per visit plus `htmx:load`): an IntersectionObserver per strip with its margins pulled to a line down the middle writes the crossing item (`data-snap-value`) into the row's hidden `garmentId`. **No touch handlers**: swiping is the browser's scrolling (`test/styling.spec.ts` checks the app has no non-passive touch or wheel listener). A tap on a peeking neighbour centres it (a click listener in the **capture** phase: the garments are boosted links, whose htmx handler on the element runs before a bubbling document listener); a tap on the chosen one opens its page. Each row posts `role`, `garmentId` and `lock` hidden fields in step; the lock is daisyUI's swap (the label is the control); styling.js's `setLocked` (a document `change` listener) owns a toggle: its row's `lock` field, `checked` mirrored into its attribute (htmx's history snapshot is innerHTML, which drops the property) and the neighbours' `inert`. Items snap one at a time (`snap-always`).
- **What a row shows is CSS off its state** (#106; `styling-row.tsx`, Tailwind `group/row` and `group/item` variants, no script): the `[data-selected]` item's plinth wears an inset ink ring (inset: the strip clips anything outside). **A locked row is frozen** (owner decision on #106): `:has(.styling-lock:checked)` gives its strip `overflow-x: hidden` and `touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom` (the page still scrolls vertically and zooms), its ring turns accent and its neighbours' plinths fade. **Its neighbours are `inert`** (#146; `itemState`): a frozen strip cannot reveal a focused neighbour (the scroll guard snapped it back and the focus sat off-screen), so they leave the tab order, the accessibility tree (the listbox offers the chosen garment alone, beside a checked "Lock <role>") and hit testing (a tap on one lands on the strip: nothing centres, nothing opens). The server renders it (`?with=`, Shuffle), so a row opened locked is right from the first paint; `setLocked` keeps it in step, and `watch()` makes a strip's next page inert as it lands (`GET /styling/garments` does not carry the lock). `inert`, not `tabindex="-1"`: that would leave unchoosable options in the accessibility tree and still reachable by a screen reader's cursor or a click. The observer never chooses in a locked row: whatever else scrolls it (a script's `scrollTo` still moves a hidden-overflow strip, which is also why centring on load works for `?with=`) is scrolled back to the chosen item. Unlocking swipes on from the same item; Shuffle keeps a locked row (it is left out of the idea) and it stays frozen. `test/styling.spec.ts` proves a wheel leaves a locked strip's `scrollLeft` alone at 390 px, and that Tab reaches only a locked row's chosen garment (`tabbedItems`; Firefox also stops on the strip itself, a scroll container), and every item once unlocked.
- **Shuffle** (`GET /styling/shuffle`, the rows posted back through `hx-include="#styling-form"`, answering `#styling-rows`): `ideasFor`'s statement and answer (`ideasColumns`, `ideasFrom`) with the locked rows as `locked`, the page's `?for=` aimed by `aimIdeas` (its day's weather and occasion, a trip's destination), `offset 0, limit 1`, seeded (`?seed=`, the day's without one; the answer carries `shuffledSeed`). Nothing fits: the rows as they were and a note. `GET /styling/row?add=<role>` ("Add row") answers the rows with one more of that role.
- **Save** (`POST /styling`, a native `PostForm`; the sheet's fields join it by `form=`): the rows' garment ids, once each, at least one (else 400). A new outfit goes through `pickTo` (the gallery's pick; with Include picks on, `saveWithPicks` instead, above): `?for=day` plans it, with `replace` it takes that entry's place (`replaceEntryOutfit`, which now takes a `name`), `for=trip:` adds it (`pickForTrip`), none saves it, or plans it on the sheet's optional day and occasion; **once however often it is posted** (`pickIdea`: the same garments are the same outfit, "Already saved"); the name blank is `ideaName`. An outfit (`outfit=`) is updated in place by `updateOutfit` (slots top to toe, the name, a day plan; a trip or `replace` is a 400), then `returnTo` (`safeReturnTo`) or the outfit. **Refused for a garment it cannot hold** (#219: archived for a new outfit, deleted, not the requester's; `OutfitGarmentsGone`, see Outfits) it answers the page again (`refusedPage`), never an error page: the requester's own wardrobe, the post's destination, capsule and outfit, its rows with the gone ones back to None, the sheet's name, day and occasion (`StylingModel.refusal.draft`), and an alert naming the garments (`data-styling-refused`), with the refusal's status (409, or 404 when one is not theirs). A hand-made post without the page's rows (`role`, `lock`) gets the error. Logs (context `Web`): `Styling save by user <id> refused (<status>): garments <id> (<status>|not theirs)...`, `Outfit styled by user <id>: ...` (the pick's line), `Outfit <id> changed in Styling by user <id>: garments ...`; the page and Shuffle at debug.
- **A shared wardrobe** (`?ownerId=`, `authorizeWardrobe` view; decided in #42): browse and Shuffle, never Save (outfits are private and hold only their owner's garments: Save reads the requester's own garments, so a grantee posting the owner's is a 404). Shuffle there is `browseIdea`: the owner's closet (`closetGarmentsSql`, so no wash or away state), rotation off, none of their saved outfits, clashes or weather. "Style this" and a capsule's Style link carry `ownerId` for a grantee; `?outfit=` never opens over a shared wardrobe.
- **Entry points**: the dock's Style, the manifest's "Style an outfit" shortcut, "Style this" (`?with=`: that garment locked, opened on the day's first idea around it; the garment page and insights' unworn rows), a capsule (`?capsule=`), the plan page (`?for=`, `&replace=`), a trip's destination, an outfit's Edit and the calendar chip (`?outfit=&returnTo=`), the Outfits page's "+ Style an outfit".

## Gotchas

- **The strips' ends are spacers, not padding** (`sizing`, #179). WebKit leaves a flex scroller's end padding out of its scrollable width when the items alone fit: a row of "No garment" and one garment did not scroll at all in Safari, so the garment could never be chosen. `::before` and `::after` spacers are `calc(50% - half an item - the gap)`, since the strip's `gap-3` also falls beside them. Items keep a fixed width (`w-28`): a percentage (`basis-2/5`) resolved against the wrong box and came out a sixth of the screen.
