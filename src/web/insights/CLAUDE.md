# Insights

`src/wardrobe/insights.ts` (pure), `src/web/insights/`, `src/web/mcp/tools/insights.ts` (#17, plan section 10). How the closet is actually used, computed on every request from the wear log; nothing stored. **The owner's own, like wears**: `GET /wardrobe/insights[?unworn=30|90|180]` (in the Wardrobe's ⋯ menu; under `/wardrobe`, so the dock marks Wardrobe) ignores `?ownerId=`, and a share never reaches it (matrix row in `authorization-outfits.spec.ts`). `?unworn=` is navigation state: anything else is 90, never a 400 (no length limit either: one answered a long value 400 before the fallback, #123).

- **Two statements** (`queries.ts`, run together by `readInsightRows`; `readInsights`, which the page and `wardrobe_stats` share, reads them over `recentWindow(today)`): `insightGarments`, one row per garment in the window's scope (the closet, `inCloset`) with its wear days ever, in the window (the last year), the last day worn, its repairs' cost up to `to` (`repairCostSql`, a scalar subquery, #151), `acquired_on`, and days since it and since `acquired_on` (Postgres `date - date` against the window's last day, today, so the pure side does no date arithmetic); and `wornPairs`, the self-join of the window's distinct (garment, day) pairs, at most 5. `InsightsWindow` (`from`, `to`, `scope`) is what the recap changes: no wear after `to` is read (a join condition, so a past year stops at its December 31). `wardrobeInsights` computes everything else from those rows. The page is the owner row plus these two (`recordQueries` in `insights.spec.ts` and `insights-seed.spec.ts`): **a new figure is computed from the rows in `insights.ts`, not a new query**, unless it needs data the rows lack.
- **Definitions** (`insights.ts` states them; the specs pin each on a known closet): the closet is `inCloset` garments, each counted once whatever its quantity (worn when any copy was); wears are distinct days. _Worn in the last N days_ (30, 90, 365): a wear today or in the N - 1 days before. _Unworn in N days_ (30, 90, 180): never worn or last worn N days ago or more, among garments owned at least N days (no `acquired_on` counts as owned long enough), never worn first, then the longest since; each with "Style this" (`styleThisUrl`, `urls.ts`: Styling's `/styling?with=` (#42), as on the garment page). _Most worn_: by wear days ever, ties the most recent first. _Least worn_: among garments owned `LEAST_WORN_MIN_OWNED_DAYS` (90) or more, leaving out the most worn. _Cost per wear_: `price` is per piece and, after "Bought it", the price paid (it overwrites the listed one), so the cost (`totalCost`, every surface's one definition) is price × quantity plus the repairs' costs (#151; once, not per copy), divided by wear days, in cents rounded to the cent; no price is left out (counted), however much its repairs cost; "Your closet cost" sums the same costs and says "repairs included"; 0 wears is "not worn yet" and never divided; best is the cheaper half at most (up to 5), worst the dearer half, so no garment is in both. _Pairs_: two closet garments worn on the same day (whichever entries held them) on at least 2 days of the last year. _Colours_: each colour's share of the whole closet's garments and of all the last year's wear days, a garment in k colours counting 1/k to each and one without a colour to `uncoloured`, the strip's plain end (strips of `.ms-swatch--<colour>` segments grown by `flex-grow`; until #123 the shares were of the coloured garments only, so a half-untagged closet read as its colours at 100%). _Categories and brands_: garments, pieces (quantity), share of the closet and of the year's wear days; brands grouped regardless of case and spaces under the first-added garment's spelling, 8 named and the rest summed. _Condition_: needs_repair and replace_soon counts, linking to the grid's "Needs attention" filter (`?attention=true`).
- **Year in review** (#26, `docs/plans/2026-09-28-yearly-recap.md`): `GET /wardrobe/recap[?year=YYYY]` (`recap-page.tsx`, `recap-period.ts`, pure `src/wardrobe/recap.ts`), in the Wardrobe's ⋯ menu (on one's own wardrobe only) and linked from insights. The same two statements over a calendar year (`from` January 1, `to` December 31 or today, `scope: 'owned'`: a garment archived since was still that year's; a repair counts by its day like a wear: every one up to `to`, before the year too, as the price is the whole price, so a past year's cost per wear is what it was on its December 31), then `yearRecap` with insights' rules (`costFigures`' best, `colourShares`' worn shares, the pairs), no statement of its own (`recap.spec.ts` counts them). **Owner-only, like insights**: the wear log never reaches a grantee, so `?ownerId=` is ignored (the schema strips it) and a grantee passing the owner's id sees their own recap (`recap.spec.ts`; matrix row in `authorization-outfits.spec.ts`). "Save image" is therefore always one's own. A first version was share-aware; the coordinator reversed it on #26. The year boundary is `todayIn(APP_TIMEZONE)`'s (`today.spec.ts` has the New Year's Eve row); `?year=` is navigation state (not a four-digit year up to this one: this one). Fewer than `RECAP_MIN_WEARS` (10) wears is the empty state; a year's additions are `acquired_on` in it (undated garments are never additions). ‹ shows only when something was worn before the year (`earlier`: lifetime wears beyond the year's), › only for a past year. **"Save image" is drawn on the device** (`public/js/recap-export.js`, the importmap's `recap-export`): Canvas 2D from the page's JSON island (`#recap-card-data`, every string already translated), the page's fonts, the light theme's tokens from a hidden `[data-theme="closet-light"]` probe (the card is light in dark mode too), the strip's segments for the swatch colours and the same-origin thumbs; drawn as the page opens, the button enabled once the PNG exists, so the tap calls `navigator.share({ files })` inside its user activation (iOS), else downloads; a card that cannot be drawn (a font that will not load only falls back) says so in `#recap-export-note` (aria-live) and leaves the button enabled to draw again. `test/recap.spec.ts` reads the PNG back (1080 × 1350, light) and forces the failure (`toBlob` answering null). Why not sharp on the server: the slim image has no fonts, and Pango ignores the woff2 we ship (the plan says more).
- **Why each statement, and its cost** (#169 audit; `npm run audit:pages -- --only '#169'`): both pages and every `?unworn=` and `?year=` are 3 statements in 2 round trips: the session's user row (the root hook's, not ours), then the two statements concurrently (`readInsightRows`' `Promise.all`, one round trip). `insightGarments` is one row per garment because every figure is a share of the whole closet (colours, categories, brands, worn in N days), so no row is unused; `wornPairs` is apart because it self-joins (garment, day) pairs, which the per-garment rows no longer hold. They share no rows, so they could be one statement (each `json_agg`'d as a scalar subquery): 3 to 2 statements, still 2 round trips, one pool connection fewer; that waits for `selectScalars` (`src/db/select-scalars.ts`, #216) rather than a second hand-rolled combiner. Plans (demo and a synthetic 10 accounts × 300 garments × 5 years of wears): the wears are an index-only scan of `garment_wear_garment_id_day_index` per garment, the repair cost (#155) an index scan of `garment_repair_garment_id_day_index` per row at about 1 µs (no rewrite: pre-aggregating would read every repair to save nothing), and `wornPairs` names the garment's owner as well as the wear's so the garments come from the owner's index, not every account's closet. Planning (0.5 to 5 ms) outweighs execution on demo data. Index findings are on #175.
- **Seed**: Theo's 13 weeks give every figure a real answer (`insights-seed.spec.ts`, the clock pinned to the anchor), and his "2026 so far" a full recap (2025: its empty state); Dana's untagged closet has no wears (the "nothing worn yet" note); Riley's is the empty state.
- **Logs**: the route logs one debug line per read (`Insights for user 3: 80 garment(s), 5 pair(s), unworn 90 days in 12 ms`; `Recap 2026 for user 3: 517 wear(s), 54 piece(s), 16 added in 9 ms`); the request line is the root hook's. The export logs to the console (`Recap: image drawn (179 KB) in 34 ms`, shared, downloaded, or why not).
